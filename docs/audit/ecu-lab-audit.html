Code audit & integration plan

Bench to Bay

What ECU Lab's physics and ECU model would need to become an accurate tuning simulator — and what it would take to put a real Nissan ECU on the other end of it.

DNiev/ecu-lab @ 2f40731 + PR #31 (peak pressure) fenugrec/nisprog fenugrec/npkern 6,457 lines src · 176 tests

01 Verdict

The model is unusually honest for a tuning game and unusually principled for a hobby codebase — real gas laws, a genuine two-VE split, no per-part horsepower bonuses, and a fingerprint test that makes physics changes a reviewed act. That discipline is the asset. It is also the thing that caps the current design.

Every combustion result comes from one algebraic step per operating point: fuel energy multiplied by three efficiency scalars. There is no crank angle, no pressure trace, no burn duration. Roughly a dozen empirical coefficients exist to paper over that missing dimension, and each one has to be hand-fitted and defended in a comment. "Extremely accurate" is not reachable by adding a fourteenth coefficient — it needs a crank-angle-resolved core that makes most of them fall out as consequences instead of inputs.

The second gap is bigger for the nisprog goal. There is no ECU in this codebase. There is a physics function with fuel arithmetic inlined in it. No scheduler, no sensor pipeline, no diagnostics, no calibration format, no bytes. A real ECU is exactly those things, and the integration target is a real ECU.

02 What already works

Worth stating plainly, because the plan below preserves all of it and none of it is common:

  • The two-VE split (veActual vs veVal) is the real basis of closed-loop VE tuning, and collapsing it is the mistake most sims make. The fuel-trim histogram has genuine signal to read.
  • Load axis is MAP, not throttle. Speed-density indexing matches what real calibration tools do.
  • Presets are falsifiable. Four production engines reproduced from published specs through shared physics, validated in CI, with the model's own failures documented in NO_PEAK_BEFORE_LIMITER rather than tolerance-widened away.
  • The fingerprint. 504 sweeps hashed; a coefficient cannot move silently. This is the single most valuable thing in the repo for what comes next — a physics rewrite is only safe because this exists.

03 Physics findings

Ranked by how much they cap accuracy, not by effort.

No crank-angle resolution — combustion is one multiply

Structural
src/sim/point.js:127–135

timingEff and afrEff are parabolas centred on an MBT estimate, multiplied into fuel energy to make IMEP. Real IMEP is ∮p dV over a cycle. Without a pressure trace the model cannot produce burn duration, real MBT, peak-pressure location, end-gas history, or exhaust temperature — so each of those is either absent or a hand-fitted proxy.

This is the root cause of at least four findings below. It is why TIMING_FALLOFF, AFR_FALLOFF, EFFICIENCY_FLOOR, OTTO_REALIZATION, the whole peak-pressure phasing block and the EGT proxy all have to exist as separate tuned numbers.

Direction A two-zone Wiebe heat-release model on a 1° crank grid. Burn duration and phasing become state; MBT stops being a correlation and becomes the timing that puts 50% mass-burned near 8–10° ATDC; IMEP comes from integration; peak pressure and its location come free. Perhaps 200 lines, and it retires more coefficients than it adds.

Knock is a threshold comparison, not an autoignition model

Accuracy
src/sim/knock.js:47–79

Knock margin is a base table plus eight additive corrections — octane, charge index, IAT, compression, head material, cylinder size, headers, exhaust — each independently fitted. Real knock is an induction-time integral: the end gas autoignites when ∫dt/τ reaches 1, with τ from an Arrhenius correlation in pressure, temperature and octane number (Douaud–Eyzat is the standard form).

The additive form cannot express the interactions that matter most to a tuner — why a hot intercooler-less charge at 20 psi is far worse than the sum of "hot" and "boosted", or why slower burn (retarded timing) gives the end gas more time to autoignite even as peak pressure falls.

Direction With a pressure trace in hand, Livengood–Wu integration over the end gas replaces most of knockThreshold. Octane becomes a real fuel property rather than a degrees-of-margin bonus, and compression, boost, IAT and charge cooling stop needing separate hand-priced terms.

Pumping work is clamped to zero under boost, and exhaust backpressure has no pressure term at all

Accuracy
src/sim/friction.js:42–44 · src/sim/airflow.js:105–108

pumpingFmepPa returns max(0, BARO − MAP). Above atmospheric that is zero, so a boosted engine pays no pumping term whatsoever. The turbine's restriction is modelled only as a 3% VE multiplier (VE_TURBINE_BACKPRESSURE).

In reality PMEP is P_exhaust − P_intake, and on a turbo engine exhaust manifold pressure commonly runs 1.5–2× boost pressure with a small or choked turbine. That is a first-order torque loss, the main reason turbine sizing matters, and a major driver of residual gas — which then drives knock. The model currently has boost as almost pure upside.

Direction Model exhaust manifold pressure from turbine flow and size, let PMEP go properly negative, and feed EMP into a residual-fraction estimate that reaches the knock model. Turbine choice starts costing what it costs in reality.

The turbo is a boost target with a spool ramp, not a matched machine

Accuracy
src/sim/manifold.js:32–39 · src/sim/hardware.js:99–115

Boost is target × spool × throttle², where spool is a linear RPM ramp. There is no compressor map, no efficiency island, no surge or choke line, no wastegate, no shaft inertia, and no energy balance between turbine and compressor. boostCeiling stands in for the entire map. The result: boost is whatever the player asks for, and the only penalty for asking too much is a fixed knock deduction.

Direction A real compressor map (pressure ratio vs corrected mass flow, with efficiency contours) plus turbine power matching. Boost then becomes an outcome of the hardware and the wastegate duty the player sets — which is what tuning a boost controller actually is.

Charge temperature ignores everything except compression

Accuracy
src/sim/thermo.js:21–26

chargeTempK returns ambient exactly whenever boost ≤ 0. A naturally aspirated engine therefore never experiences heat soak, port heating, hot-side radiation, or residual-gas mixing — the charge at IVC is meaningfully hotter than IAT in every real engine, and that difference is what the knock model needs. Ambient itself is a fixed 298 K at fixed sea-level pressure, so altitude and weather — both first-order tuning variables, and both things a real ECU corrects for — do not exist.

Direction Split IAT (sensor) from trapped-charge temperature (physics): mix intake charge with residual mass at exhaust temperature, add a wall heat-transfer term. Make baro and ambient inputs, then the ECU model can have the baro correction real ECUs have.

Fuel delivery is idealised in the three places tuners actually get bitten

Fidelity
src/sim/point.js:99–110 · src/sim/constants.js:45

Injector deadtime is one constant (1.0 ms) — in reality it is a table against battery voltage and fuel pressure, and getting it wrong is the classic cause of idle and low-load mixture errors after an injector swap. There is no small-pulse nonlinearity, so big injectors carry none of their real low-load penalty. Fuel pressure and its differential across the rail are absent, so a returnless system under boost never leans out.

Direction Deadtime as a voltage table, a low-pulse linearity curve per injector, and rail differential pressure as a modelled quantity. All three are cheap and all three are things a tuner must get right.

MAF error is a flat scalar, so the rescale lesson is easier than reality

Fidelity
src/sim/sweep.js:61–66

A bigger intake multiplies true airflow by 0.90 at every flow rate. Real MAF error from a housing change is a shape change in the transfer function — often a few percent mid-range and much worse at the extremes — which is precisely why a single scalar cannot fix it and why real tuners rescale the curve point by point. The app teaches the scalar workflow because the model only has a scalar to teach.

Direction Make the MAF a voltage-vs-flow transfer function, and hardware distort its shape. The rescale then becomes the multi-point job it really is — and matches the MAF table you would edit in a Nissan ROM.

Ideal-cycle efficiency uses an exponent that is not the ideal cycle's

Cosmetic
src/sim/engine.js:142

ottoIdeal = 1 − 1/CR^0.35. The ideal Otto cycle uses γ−1 = 0.4 for air; 0.35 is a reasonable stand-in for real burned-gas properties, but it is presented as the ideal cycle and then multiplied by a second realisation factor of 0.685. Two fudge factors stacked where one physical statement would do. Harmless today, confusing to anyone deriving from it.

Direction A Wiebe core makes this moot — indicated efficiency becomes an output. Until then, say plainly in the comment that 0.35 is an empirical γ−1 for burned gas, not the textbook value.

04 The ECU is missing

This is the finding that matters most for where you are going. The app's ECU is about forty lines: a fuel calculation inlined in evaluatePoint, plus idle control, DFCO, a rev limiter and fuel trims in live.js. There is no ECU object, no separable ECU module, and nothing that resembles ECU architecture. Grep the simulation layer and you will not find a diagnostic trouble code, a task scheduler, a transient fuel term, or a single byte of calibration data.

The load-axis decision comes first

Everything downstream depends on it. ECU Lab is a GM/HP Tuners-shaped speed-density sim — VE indexed by RPM × MAP. Nissan SH705x ECUs, the ones nisprog talks to, are mass-airflow machines: the MAF reads air mass, Tp (basic injection pulse width) is derived as K × Qa/N, and fuel, timing and knock tables are indexed by Tp and engine speed.

If a player is going to learn on the simulator and then tune a real 350Z, the simulator has to teach the axis they will actually meet. That argues for making the ECU model pluggable — a speed-density personality and a Nissan MAF/Tp personality over the same physics — rather than converting the app to one and losing the other.

05 What proves accuracy today

The fingerprint proves stability — that numbers did not move unnoticed. The preset tests prove plausibility at four points: peak power and peak torque for four engines, within ±5% and ±10%. Nothing in the repo compares the model to a measured curve, a real datalog, or a second implementation.

That is the right test suite for a teaching toy and an insufficient one for "extremely accurate". A model can hit four published peaks and still have the wrong curve shape between them, the wrong response to a timing change, and the wrong knock onset — none of which any current test would notice.

The missing asset is a validation corpus. Real dyno curves and real datalogs, checked into the repo, with error metrics computed in CI: RMSE on the torque curve rather than its peak, predicted-vs-logged AFR, predicted-vs-logged knock retard. That corpus is also the thing that turns the nisprog integration from a feature into an accuracy engine — see phase 3.

06 nisprog and npkern, as they actually are

Read the source rather than the summary, because three properties decide the whole integration design.

What they give you

npkern is a small program uploaded into the ECU's RAM that takes over the running ECU and serves an extended ISO 14230 command set over the K line. The commands that matter:

SIDCommandWhat it unlocks for you
0x23ReadMemByAddress, ≤251 BLive datalogging from ECU RAM — the accuracy flywheel
0xBDFast dump, ROM or EEPROMWhole-ROM read at ~5.4 kB/s vs ~100 B/s without the kernel
0xBCUnprotect / erase block / write 128 B + CRCReflash, selectively by erase block
0xBESet baud (BRR divisor), CRC ROM chunks62500 bps comms; fast verify without a full re-read
0x3DWriteMemByAddress — RAM onlyRAM patching; not a route to live map editing

nisprog is the host side: connection, the Nissan seed–key security algorithms (genkey1/genkey2 in nis_backend.c), kernel upload, and the flash workflow. It builds on freediag for the K-line transport.

Constraint 1 — licence

Both are GPLv3; ECU Lab is MIT. Linking nisprog's code into the app makes the whole app GPLv3. That is a real decision with real consequences, not a formality, and it has to be made before any code is written. The architecture in the next section is shaped primarily by this.

Constraint 2 — a browser cannot drive a K line

ISO 14230 needs a 5-baud or fast-init wake pattern with tens-of-milliseconds precision, then byte-level timing (P1–P4) inside a few ms, then a mid-session baud change to 62500. Web Serial can open a port, but a JavaScript event loop cannot be trusted with initialisation timing, and nisprog's "dumb" interface support bit-bangs init through DTR/RTS. A native helper process is not optional.

Constraint 3 — flash wear forbids the obvious product

npkern's own README: these parts are rated around one hundred write cycles, and it explicitly warns against live-tuning applications. The natural feature — "edit a map, hit flash, drive, repeat" — would consume an ECU's rated endurance in an afternoon. Any real-hardware feature has to be designed around a flash budget, with iteration happening in the simulator and only validated tunes reaching the car.

07 Proposed architecture

One boundary does three jobs at once: it keeps GPL code out of the MIT app, puts real-time serial work in a process that can do it, and gives the dangerous operations a single choke point to gate.

Two separate programs exchanging messages over a socket is the arrangement least likely to make the web app a derivative work — but this is a call for a lawyer, not for me, and the honest summary is that the FSF and industry practice disagree at the margins. Get an opinion before shipping, not after. The fallback if the answer is unfavourable is clean: relicense the desktop distribution as GPLv3 and keep the web simulator MIT.

A side benefit worth naming: everything in phases 1 and 2 below runs entirely in the browser with no bridge, no hardware and no risk — and that is where most of the user value is.

08 The plan

Six phases. The order is deliberate: each one is useful shipped alone, and each earns the right to the next. Nothing touches a real ECU until phase 4.

0FOUNDATION

Give the ECU a body

Extract the ECU from the physics. A src/ecu/ module with an explicit scheduler (10 ms / 100 ms / per-event tasks), a sensor pipeline with conversion and rationality checks, and fuel, spark, idle and knock as separate strategies. The physics keeps its current interface; the ECU becomes something you can swap.

Then define an ECU personality: the object that says how load is computed, what the tables are indexed by, and how values are quantised. Ship two — the current speed-density one, and a Nissan MAF/Tp one.

Gate The fingerprint is unchanged for the speed-density personality. A refactor that moves a number is a bug.
1PHYSICS

Crank-angle core

Wiebe heat release on a 1° grid; IMEP by integration; Livengood–Wu end-gas autoignition replacing the knock threshold; EMP and honest PMEP; residual mixing into charge temperature. Retire the coefficients this makes redundant, and let the fingerprint report exactly what moved.

This is the phase that decides whether "extremely accurate" is a slogan or a claim. It is also the one that most needs phase 2 to exist first, so run them together if you can.

Gate Every preset still lands within its published tolerance, with the new core and fewer free parameters than before.
2EVIDENCE

Validation corpus

Check real dyno curves and real datalogs into the repo. Add a harness that replays a log through the model and reports error metrics — torque-curve RMSE, AFR error, knock-retard agreement — as CI output alongside the fingerprint. Accuracy stops being an opinion and becomes a number that can regress.

Gate Published error metrics for at least three engines, with the worst case stated in the README rather than buried.
3BYTES

ROM domain model — offline, no hardware

A definition format (map address, axes, scaling, units — the job RomRaider defs and TunerPro XDFs do), a binary loader, checksum handling, and map editors that read a real ROM in the browser. Open a real 350Z ROM and see its real maps, with the simulator predicting what an edit does.

No serial port, no risk, and it is probably the single highest-value feature in this plan. It also forces the quantisation work from phase 0 to be real.

Gate Load a stock ROM, edit a map, write it out, and have the bytes differ only where intended with the checksum correct.
4HARDWARE

Garage Bridge — read only, first

Ship the bridge with connect, identify, and read — ROM dump and the 0x23 RAM-read datalogging stream. No erase, no write, not even behind a flag. A read-only release proves the transport, the timing and the hardware matrix while it is impossible to brick anything.

Then wire the datalog stream into the phase 2 harness. Now every user with a car is contributing validation data, and the simulator's accuracy improves because people are using it.

Gate A dump that byte-matches a known-good ROM, on at least two ECU types, from at least two adapters.
5WRITE

Guided reflash

Flash last, wrapped in the safety design the constraints demand: mandatory original dump held before the first write, verify-after-write by CRC, a visible flash-cycle budget per ECU against the ~100-cycle rating, power-loss warnings, and a recovery path documented before the feature ships.

This is also where "after learning" becomes real: gate write access behind completion of the simulator curriculum. The person who has never seen what detonation does to a piston in the sim does not get to send bytes to their car.

Gate Legal opinion on the licence boundary, and a written recovery procedure, both landed before the first public build.

10 Risks worth naming now

  • Bricking someone's ECU. The mitigations in phase 5 are the minimum, not a nice-to-have. A recovery procedure has to exist before the first write ships.
  • Flash endurance is a hard budget. Design the product around it from the start; retrofitting a cycle counter after users have burned their ECUs is too late.
  • Legality varies. Reflashing may be illegal on road vehicles in the user's jurisdiction and will affect emissions compliance. Say so in the product, once, clearly, and do not bury it.
  • Licence drift. The boundary only holds if it is maintained. One convenience import of GPL code into the web app collapses it.
  • Scope. Phases 0–3 are a substantial project on their own and deliver most of the value. Phases 4–5 are where the risk concentrates. It is entirely reasonable to ship 0–3 and stop to reassess.
